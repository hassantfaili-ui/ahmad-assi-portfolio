Bowesville is a station area rather than a neighbourhood yet, which is the interesting part. The area plan our team produced for Earl Armstrong sets out the blocks, the uses and the storey heights around a new light rail stop, colour coded from single storey housing up to five storey commercial, with parks, corridors and a community farm threaded through it.

Serenade Park is my own piece of that: the public open space at the centre. Rather than one lawn, it is a sequence of grounds with their own names and their own jobs, arranged around Hydro-Stream Lake. Tri-Spectra holds the north edge as the main gathering plaza, the Arch-Flora Pavilion sits in the planted ground to its west, Timberwoods keeps the treed edge, and a promenade runs down the lake to The Apex at the south.

Naming the parts was not decoration. A park that reads as one undifferentiated green is used as a shortcut; a park made of places people can arrange to meet at gets used as a destination.
