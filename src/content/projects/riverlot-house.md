The lot is 40 metres wide and drops four metres to the river. Building across the slope would have meant fill and a retaining wall. Building along it meant a long, thin house, one room deep, which turned out to be the more interesting problem.

Every habitable room faces south. In January the sun sits low enough to reach the back wall of the kitchen, roughly nine metres in, and the overhang is cut to stop that same sun in late June. The north side carries the circulation, the services and the storage, and it is deliberately thick: a buffer rather than a facade.

The envelope is a 400 millimetre assembly with a service cavity inside the air barrier, so nobody drills through it later to hang a television. That detail cost more than it should have to defend, and it is the one I would fight for again.
